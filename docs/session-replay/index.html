Session Replay

Record visits to your website, watch them back beside their events, and see where people click.

Session Replay records what a visitor saw and did on your website — page changes, scrolling, clicks, typing — and plays it back like a video, with the visit's events alongside. Use it when a report tells you what happened and you want to see how.

Start recording

Recording is part of Kestrel's web SDK. Turn it on with two options where you initialise the SDK, next to your token and api_host: the share of sessions to record, and where to load the recorder from — your Kestrel address followed by /lib/.

record_sessions_percent: 100,
lib_base_path: "https://ingest.your-company.kestrel.rivero.co.in/lib/",

record_sessions_percent is 0 by default, so nothing is recorded until you set it. 100 records every session; 10 records about one session in ten. The recorder is served by your own Kestrel deployment — the SDK loads no code from anywhere else.

The quickest way to get the right code is the Set Up Guide: turn on Session Replay in its install step and copy the snippet (see Setting up Kestrel).

What is recorded, and what is hidden

Replays are built for privacy first. By default:

  • All text and every input is masked. Visitors' words show as placeholders, never as what they typed or read. Password, email, phone and hidden fields are always masked.
  • Images, video and audio are not recorded; the player shows an empty box of the same size.

To change what is shown, use the SDK's options when you initialise it:

OptionWhat it does
record_mask_all_text, record_mask_all_inputsSet to false to record text or input values by default.
record_mask_text_selector, record_unmask_text_selectorCSS selectors for text to always hide, or always show.
record_mask_input_selector, record_unmask_input_selectorThe same, for form fields.
record_block_selectorElements left out of the recording entirely. Default: img, video, audio.

You can also mark elements in your HTML: the class mp-mask hides an element's text, and mp-block leaves the element out.

How long a replay runs

  • After 30 minutes without activity a replay ends, and the recorder starts a new one.
  • No single recording runs longer than 24 hours.
  • Replays are kept for 30 days, then deleted.

Watching replays

Open Session Replay from the left navigation. The list on the left shows recent replays, newest first; filter it by event, by user, or by event and user properties to find the visits you care about. Pick one to play it, with its events listed beside the player — click an event to jump to that moment.

Replays are also a click away elsewhere: Watch Replays on a user's profile plays that person's sessions, and reports offer the replays behind a data point.

Save a filtered list as a playlist to come back to the same set of replays, or add it to a board.

Heatmaps

A heatmap shows where visitors clicked on a page, drawn over a replay of that page. To collect the data, turn on Record Heatmap Data in the replay settings (below), or set record_heatmap_data: true in the SDK.

Settings

Organisation Owners and Admins set recording rules per project under Organization Settings › Session Replay:

  • Sampling — the share of sessions each project records, and conditional rules that record more when a given event, property value or URL appears. Rules only ever add recordings.
  • Advanced — per platform: the shortest and longest recording kept, the inactivity timeout, and whether to record heatmap data.

The SDK follows these settings when it is initialised with remote_settings_mode; otherwise the options in your code apply.

Not seeing replays?

  • Check that record_sessions_percent is above 0 and that lib_base_path points at your Kestrel address, ending in /lib/.
  • A replay appears once the visitor's first batch of recording arrives — usually within seconds. Leave the page open a little while when testing.
  • Content blockers in some browsers stop analytics scripts, and with them the recorder.
