Growth primer

How many people get value from your product over time, and the growth reports on the Company KPIs board.

Growth is the number of people getting value from your product, over time. It has two halves: bringing new people in, and keeping the ones you have. A product grows when the people it gains each period outnumber the people it loses — which is why growth is read alongside engagement and retention, not on its own.

Active users

The most common growth numbers count active users: distinct people who did something in a window of time.

MetricWho it counts
DAU — daily active usersDistinct people active on each day.
WAU — weekly active usersDistinct people active in the 7 days ending on each day.
MAU — monthly active usersDistinct people active in the 30 days ending on each day.

What makes these useful is what you call "active". Count everyone who opened the app and the number flatters you; count people who reached your value moment and it tells you how many people your product actually served. In Insights, choose the event, measure it by Unique Users, and pick the aggregation in its settings (see Measurements in Insights).

DAU divided by MAU is a quick read of habit: the closer to 1, the more of your monthly users come back day after day. What a good ratio is depends on how often your product is meant to be used.

The board's growth reports

What are the DAU and WAU?

A line chart of daily and weekly active users, both counted on the value moment. Watch the gap between them: a widening gap means more people use the product some days a week rather than every day.

How many new users are signing up?

Weekly first-time sign-ups over the last three months, compared with the same weeks a year earlier, so seasonal swings don't read as growth or decline.

Which channels bring the most sign-ups?

Sign-ups each week broken down by each user's latest campaign source (utm_source [last touch]). Judge a channel by the users who stay as well as by how many sign up — break a retention report down the same way — since a channel that brings many people who soon leave helps less than it seems.

Reading growth well

  • Separate new from returning. A flat active-user line can hide strong sign-ups masking poor retention, or the reverse.
  • Mind the window. A 30-day count reacts slowly: a change you made last week will take weeks to show fully in MAU.
  • Count only what you trust. Bots, test accounts and internal users inflate every growth number; filter them out, or define the value moment so they can't reach it.
