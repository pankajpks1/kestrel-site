Attribution

Credit each conversion to the touchpoints that led up to it, with the model of your choice.

An ordinary breakdown splits a conversion by the value it carried when it happened. An attributed breakdown looks back instead: it finds the values the person was exposed to before converting — the campaign that brought them, the pages they landed on — and shares the conversion's credit among them. Use it to answer "which channels drive our purchases?" when the purchase event itself doesn't say.

Adding one

  1. In an Insights report, open Breakdown and go to Computed.
  2. Choose Attributed by…, then the property to credit — utm_source, for example.
  3. Pick the model on each metric, and the lookback window on the breakdown (both below).

A report can have one attributed breakdown, alongside ordinary ones.

Touchpoints

A touchpoint is any of the person's events that has a value for the property, at or before the conversion. The conversion's own value counts too. A conversion with no touchpoint in its window lands in (not set).

The lookback window limits how far back touchpoints are read:

  • Days — from 1 to 366 days before the conversion; 30 by default. A touch exactly that many days earlier still counts.
  • 1 Session — only touchpoints in the same session as the conversion (see Sessions). Good for landing and exit pages.

Filter Touchpoints lets you leave out values that shouldn't earn credit — a direct or internal source, say. Excluded values are dropped before the model runs, whatever their capitalisation, and the report's title notes what was excluded.

Models

Each conversion has one unit of credit; the model decides how it is shared among the touchpoints, in time order.

ModelCredit goes to
Last Touch (default)The most recent touchpoint, all of it.
First TouchThe earliest touchpoint in the window, all of it.
ParticipationEvery distinct value seen, a full unit each. Totals are therefore larger than the number of conversions: a purchase after three campaigns counts once for each.
LinearEvery touchpoint equally.
Time DecayRecent touchpoints more than old ones. A touchpoint's weight halves for every 7 days between it and the conversion, and the weights are then scaled to add up to one.
U-Shaped40% to the first touchpoint, 40% to the last, and 20% shared by the ones between.
J-Shaped20% to the first, 60% to the last, 20% shared between.
Inverse J-Shaped60% to the first, 20% to the last, 20% shared between.
CustomYour own first, middle and last percentages, saved under a name you give it.

For the position-based models (U, J, Inverse J and Custom): a single touchpoint takes all the credit, and with exactly two the first and last shares are rescaled to add up to 100% — U-Shaped gives 50% and 50%.

What the numbers mean

  • Total Events adds up the credit each value earned. With a fractional model (Linear, Time Decay, the shaped ones) a value can have a fractional count.
  • Sum of a property gives each value its share of every conversion's amount — half the credit, half the revenue.
  • Unique Users credits each person once per time bucket, from either their first or their last conversion in it. Choose which on the metric: First Segment or Last Segment.

First Touch, Last Touch and Participation give whole units of credit, so they work with every measurement. The fractional models work with Total Events, Unique Users and Sum.

Attribution is computed from every event in the range, person by person — never from a sample — so the same report always gives the same numbers.
