Engagement primer

What engagement measures, how to read it in Kestrel, and the reports on the Company KPIs board.

Engagement is how much of your product's value people actually take up: how many of them reach your value moment, how often, and how quickly after they arrive. It is the earliest of the three signals on the Company KPIs board — engaged users are the ones who stay (retention), and users who stay are what growth is built on.

Measure the outcome, not the visit

Opening the app or viewing a page says someone showed up, not that they were helped. Measure engagement on the value moment — the order placed, the message sent, the report shared — and the numbers start to mean something. If several actions count, combine them into one custom event first.

The board's engagement reports

How many users reach the value moment?

A line chart of the value moment each day, as two metrics: Unique Users (how many people did it) and Total Events (how many times it happened). Read them together: total events rising while unique users stay flat means the same people are doing it more — deeper engagement, not wider. To watch frequency directly, measure the value moment by Frequency per User (see Measurements in Insights).

Who are the most engaged users?

The template creates an Engaged Users cohort: people who reached the value moment at least 3 times in the last 30 days. Open it to see who they are, break your other reports down by it, or compare it with everyone else. Change the threshold to fit your product's rhythm — 3 times a month is a lot for a tax app and very little for a messaging app.

What is the activation rate?

A funnel from sign-up to the value moment, completed within 30 days. Its conversion rate is your activation rate: the share of new users who get to value at all. It is often the single most useful number for a young product, because every later metric depends on it. Break it down by sign-up source or platform to find where activation is weakest.

What happens to users who don't activate?

A Flows report starting at sign-up shows the steps people take next. Follow the paths that never reach the value moment to see where they stall or leave — those are the places to simplify.

Reading engagement well

  • Compare like with like. A weekly rhythm makes daily numbers noisy; switch the chart to weekly before drawing conclusions.
  • Watch the ratio, not only the count. More engaged users can simply mean more users. The share of active users who reach the value moment is the better health signal.
  • Segment before you decide. An average can hide a group doing very well and another doing badly; a breakdown by plan, platform or acquisition channel usually tells the real story.
