Segment limits

Why a broken-down report shows at most 3,000 segments, which ones you see, and how to reach the rest.

When you break a report down by one or more properties, every distinct combination of values becomes a segment — one row in the table and, usually, one line or bar in the chart. A breakdown on a property with many values (user IDs, URLs, search terms) can produce far more segments than anyone can read, so Kestrel shows at most 3,000 segments per report.

When you hit the limit

If a report reaches 3,000 segments, a notice appears above the table. The numbers you see are still exact — Kestrel never samples or estimates — but segments beyond the first 3,000 are left out of the table and the chart.

Which segments you see

Kestrel keeps the 3,000 largest segments, ranked by the metric's total over the whole date range, largest first. For example, with Total Events broken down by Page URL, you see the 3,000 most-visited URLs in the selected period.

  • For Frequency per User metrics, segments are ranked by the measured value.
  • Segments are ranked once for the whole range, so the same segments appear on every day of a line chart.

Seeing hidden segments

  • Filter first. Add a filter that narrows the report — a country, a platform, a date range — and the segments you care about move inside the limit.
  • Break down by fewer properties. Each extra breakdown multiplies the number of combinations. You can use up to five breakdowns per report.
  • Group values. For number properties, use custom buckets to turn thousands of values into a few ranges. For text, a custom property or a lookup table can map many values into a few groups.
  • Export to CSV. The notice's Export button downloads the report's segments as a CSV file. Exporting needs the Analyst role or higher.
