Impact answers "did this launch change anything?". You pick the event that means a user adopted your launch, and the events you hope it moved. Kestrel splits users into adopters (did the launch event in your date range) and non-adopters (didn't), lines their activity up around the moment of adoption, and shows how far the adopters' behaviour moved beyond what the non-adopters' movement predicts.

Open it from the Apps menu.

Setting up a report

  1. Launch event — the event that marks adoption, with filters on its properties if only some of those events count.
  2. Impacted events — up to 20 events you expect the launch to affect. For each, choose what to measure: Total Events, Unique Users, or the Sum or Average of a numeric property.
  3. User definition — count users who did only the impacted event, or users who did any event in the date range.
  4. Filters — narrow every event the report reads by event property, or to a cohort or segment.
  5. Pick the date range and the window (the number of days before and after adoption to compare, 1 to 90, 15 by default).

How days are lined up

Everything is measured in relative days, in the project's time zone. An adopter's day 0 is the day of their first launch event in the date range. A non-adopter has no adoption day, so they use the day the first adopter launched.

A group's value on a day is its metric events that day divided by the number of users in the group. The pre-launch rate is the average of the days before day 0 (−window … −1) and the post-launch rate is the average of the days after it (1 … window). Day 0 itself is in neither. Counts are exact, and rates are rounded to two decimals.

Reading the numbers

NumberWhat it means
Pre-launch relative deltaHow far adopters were from non-adopters before the launch: (adopters − non-adopters) ÷ non-adopters.
Unobserved counterfactual of adopters (UCA)What the adopters' post-launch rate would probably have been without the launch: the non-adopters' post-launch rate, moved by the pre-launch relative delta.
Post-launch relative deltaHow far adopters were from non-adopters after the launch, using the actual adopters' rate.
Impact deltaThe adopters' actual post-launch rate minus the UCA. This is the launch's estimated effect.
Impact relative deltaThe impact delta divided by the UCA.
ConfidenceHow unlikely it is that the adopters' change is the same as the non-adopters' change by chance. Kestrel pairs each day after launch with the same distance before it, compares the two groups' average changes with a z-test, and shows one minus the p-value.

A division by zero shows as 0. When the non-adopters' post-launch rate is 0, the UCA is 0 and both relative deltas show 0.00%.

The summary card at the top states the impact in words, and the chart shows the day-by-day lines for both groups with dashed pre- and post-launch averages and the day 0 band.

Causal Impact

Adopters often differ from non-adopters before the launch even happens, which can distort the comparison. Causal Impact adjusts for this:

  1. For each user, Kestrel counts every other event they did in the days before day 0 and keeps the 10 that best separate adopters from the rest.
  2. It fits a regularised logistic regression to estimate each user's likelihood of adopting.
  3. Users are split into 10 groups by that likelihood, and adopters are compared with non-adopters inside each group.
  4. The group differences are combined into the average treatment effect (all users) and the average treatment effect on the treated (adopters), each with a 95% confidence interval.

The same data always gives the same answer. Causal Impact needs enough data: at least 200 users, at least 10 usable events, and at least 3 adopters and 3 non-adopters in every one of the 10 groups. If any of these fails, the report says there isn't enough data instead of showing a result.
